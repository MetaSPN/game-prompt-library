THE 6 CREATOR GAMES Framework:

1. Identity / Canon
Core question: "Who should people become—and who should they study?"
Signals: References to thinkers, frameworks for seeing the world, "becoming" language, worldview-shaping, canonical recommendations
Success: Audience adopts your frame, cites you as foundational, identity shifts

2. Idea / Play Mining
Core question: "What can we extract and apply right now?"
Signals: Listicles, tactical breakdowns, "here's what works," actionable takeaways, templates, swipe files
Success: Audience implements immediately, gets quick results, returns for more tactics

3. Model / Understanding
Core question: "How does this actually work?"
Signals: Explanations of mechanisms, mental models, "under the hood" analysis, first-principles reasoning, diagrams
Success: Audience understands deeply, can reason from principles, transfers knowledge to new situations

4. Performance / Coaching
Core question: "How do you get better results?"
Signals: Directive advice, skill-building, accountability framing, "do this then this," measurable improvement
Success: Audience improves measurably, attributes growth to you

5. Meaning / Sensemaking
Core question: "What does this mean for how we live?"
Signals: Interpretation, wisdom, sitting with questions, contextualizing experience, philosophical reflection
Success: Audience feels oriented, makes sense of chaos, finds significance

6. Network / Coordination
Core question: "Who should be connected—and how?"
Signals: Introductions, community-building, "scene" creation, gathering people, facilitating connections
Success: Audience connects to each other, collaborations form, network effects compound


Using the 6 creator games framework:

1. What game are they primarily playing?
2. How consistent are they? (Do they drift?)
3. What signals reveal their game? (Specific language, structure, CTAs)
4. What do they explicitly NOT do? (Which games are they avoiding?)
5. What can I learn from their discipline that I could apply to my own game?
